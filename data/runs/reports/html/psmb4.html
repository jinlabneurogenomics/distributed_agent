Psmb4 perturbation report: proteasome-insufficiency with selective neuronal vulnerability

Psmb4 is the strongest perturbation in this screen and produces a coherent on-pathway proteasome-insufficiency signature. The development-relevant readout is not generic toxicity but a cell-type-selective combination of proteasome compensation, stress-program activation, and neuronal-state erosion.

Executive Summary

Biology

Psmb4 produced 9,950 significant DE events across 20 assayed cell groups, involving 3,694 unique genes and ranking 1st of 2,046 perturbations by DE burden. The recurring pattern is induction of proteasome and stress genes with repression of neuronal and synaptic genes.

Clinical

For development, Psmb4 looks more like a causal disease-and-safety node than a tractable inhibition target. The supplied literature supports human proteasomopathy relevance, while this neuronal dataset argues that CNS biology may be driven by proteostasis collapse without a strong canonical interferon program.

Decision Signal

Do not treat this dataset as support for direct Psmb4 inhibition. The cleaner opportunities are rescue/restoration strategies, biomarker design, and CNS de-risking for any program that could suppress proteasome capacity.

1Perturbation Overview

Psmb4 is one of the largest effects in the screen. At FDR < 0.05 and |logFC| ≥ 0.25, it generated 9,950 significant DE events across 20 cell groups with results and 3,694 unique genes, ranking 1st among 2,046 perturbations. The signal is balanced rather than one-directional, with 5,169 upregulated and 4,781 downregulated significant events.

  • Psmb4 itself was not significantly depleted in any cell group after multiple-testing correction.
  • There were 2,019 genes with at least one significant up event and 1,693 with at least one significant down event.

2Cell-Type Specificity

The response is sharply cell-type selective. Strongest burden appears in midbrain, hypothalamic/subcortical, and Pvalb-related populations, whereas hippocampal glutamatergic groups are relatively buffered. The heterogeneity is not explained simply by perturbed-cell number: 012 MEA LA CA1 DG Glut and 017 CA3 CA2-FC DG Glut had 42 and 53 perturbed cells but 0 significant DE genes, while 008 L2-3 IT ENT PPP RSP Glut had only 22 perturbed cells and still produced 190 DE genes.

  • Top groups were 155 MB Glut (1,954), 110 CNU-HYa HY MM Glut (1,614), and 191 MB P MY GABA (1,494).
  • Across the 20 assayed groups, DE burden correlated only modestly with perturbed-cell count (Pearson r=0.44; Spearman ρ=0.17).

3Molecular Signature

The recurrent signature resolves into a compact set of modules rather than diffuse stress noise. A 180-gene recurrent panel was significant in at least 8 Psmb4-responsive groups; 75 of those genes were recurrently up and 105 recurrently down. The up arm is dominated by proteasome compensation and proteostasis stress, while the down arm reflects loss of mature neuronal identity.

  • Proteasome compensation is recurrent and directional, arguing for on-pathway proteasome insufficiency.
  • The neuronal-loss arm affects excitability, synaptic vesicle, receptor, and neuron–glia communication genes.

4Pathways and Biological Interpretation

The pathway-level readout is a three-part state transition: compensatory proteasome biogenesis, proteotoxic/ISR stress, and neuronal functional shutdown. Strong responder groups show all three components together, which is more specific than a generic essential-gene signature. Resilient hippocampal groups often preserve the same direction for core markers but at lower magnitude and without significance.

6Literature Context and Novelty

The supplied literature narrative and the dataset agree on the core consequences of proteasome loss: compensatory proteasome biogenesis, stress-program activation, autophagy-like engagement, and neuronal/synaptic disruption. The main difference is that classic peripheral PRAAS/PSMB4 literature emphasizes interferonopathy, whereas these neurons do not show a convincing canonical type I interferon program. The most novel aspect of the screen is therefore the combination of strong cell-state selectivity and neuron-centric synaptic de-differentiation without robust IFN signaling.

  • PSMB4 is described in the supplied narrative as a broadly expressed constitutive proteasome subunit, so vulnerability likely reflects proteostasis reserve rather than simple expression specificity.
  • Direct statements about dopaminergic neurons remain limited because 215 MB Dopa had no Psmb4 row in this file.

7Drug Attempts

The supplied clinical-extension narrative supports PSMB4 as a real disease gene but not a mature direct drug target. Publicly described progress is concentrated in downstream management of proteasomopathy/interferonopathy phenotypes rather than direct selective PSMB4 engagement or correction. For portfolio decisions, the key split is between successful or partially successful downstream phenotype control and the near-absence of credible direct-target chemistry or clinical programs.

  • No approved therapy directly targets PSMB4.
  • The oncology story is a dependency story, not a chemistry-enabled target story.

8Clinical and Translational Relevance

For development, the crucial directional conclusion is that reduced Psmb4 function in neurons looks like a liability state, not a therapeutic mimic. The dataset supports a split translational model: systemic PSMB4 loss can map onto proteasomopathy/interferonopathy biology, but CNS manifestations may instead reflect neuron-intrinsic proteostasis collapse with synaptic shutdown. That argues for rescue/restoration strategies, separate peripheral and CNS biomarkers, and explicit CNS safety de-risking for any approach that lowers proteasome capacity.

  • Best-supported therapeutic direction: restoration or rescue rather than inhibition.
  • Potential downstream translational handles from the dataset include Tnfrsf12a/Fn14 and CLU.
  • Peripheral IFN scores may still matter for systemic disease, but they look weak as standalone CNS pharmacodynamic markers in this dataset.

Judge Scores

Novelty6
Novelty rationale

Most of the core signal recapitulates known proteasome-loss biology: compensatory proteasome gene induction, stress/ISR features, autophagy-like responses, and loss of neuronal/synaptic programs. The more novel elements are the in vivo brain single-cell context, the strong cell-type-selective vulnerability pattern, and especially the reported absence of a canonical type I interferon program despite PSMB4's literature link to PRAAS/CANDLE interferonopathy. Those are meaningful extensions rather than a major paradigm shift, and the novelty is tempered by the DE-only design, lack of robust self-transcript depletion, and limited mechanistic validation.

Biological Significance7
Biological Significance rationale

If the observations are valid, they are biologically important. Psmb4 perturbation appears to produce one of the strongest on-pathway signatures in the screen and links proteasome insufficiency to broad neuronal-state remodeling, including stress activation and repression of synaptic/excitability genes, with marked heterogeneity across brain cell classes. That could matter for selective vulnerability in proteasome disorders and neurodegeneration. The score is not higher because the evidence is transcriptomic only and does not directly demonstrate proteasome activity loss, neuronal dysfunction, or cell death in vivo.

Clinical Interest5
Clinical Interest rationale

The reports make PSMB4 clinically relevant as a disease and safety-biology gene: there is real human genetic support from PRAAS/CANDLE-spectrum disease, and the distinction between peripheral interferon biology and possible neuron-intrinsic CNS phenotypes is translationally interesting. However, they also argue convincingly that PSMB4 is a weak direct therapeutic target: it is a broadly expressed structural proteasome subunit, public chemistry is sparse, systemic or CNS inhibition looks safety-limited, and current clinical precedent is indirect downstream management such as JAK inhibition or HSCT. That supports moderate translational interest centered on rescue/restoration or biomarker strategies rather than a strong conventional target program.
