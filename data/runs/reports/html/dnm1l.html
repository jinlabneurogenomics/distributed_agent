Dnm1l perturbation reveals selective subcortical mitochondrial stress

In mouse-brain Perturb-seq, Dnm1l knockdown does not produce uniform neuronal toxicity. Instead it triggers a selective mitochondrial integrated stress response concentrated in subcortical neuron classes, with one thalamic glutamatergic state showing additional loss of synaptic and mitochondrial-encoded transcripts. For development strategy, the signature looks more like an on-target CNS liability pattern than a cleanly neuroprotective consequence of target suppression.

Executive Summary

Biology

Direct dataset evidence points to a coherent Dnm1l-loss program dominated by CHAC1/DDIT3/ASNS-centered ISR activation, amino-acid transport, and mitochondrial one-carbon metabolism. The response is sharply cell-type selective: only 5 of 21 annotated groups show FDR-significant downstream DE, and the strongest thalamic glutamatergic responder also loses neuronal/synaptic and mt-encoded respiratory transcripts.

Clinical

The supplied report places mouse Dnm1l in the context of human DNM1L/DRP1, a bona fide neurologic disease gene with broad tissue risk. These data strengthen concern that chronic CNS target suppression is itself stress-inducing, while leaving room for mutation-specific rescue or short-course, pathology-selective modulation as more defensible strategies.

Decision Signal

For a drug developer, the highest-value use of this dataset is as a mechanistic and safety filter: compounds or modalities that phenocopy the Dnm1l signature in neurons should be treated as liability signals. The more promising paths are rare-disease stratification, downstream rescue, or tissue-targeted modulation that avoids sustained brain-wide DNM1L loss.

1Perturbation Overview

Dnm1l perturbation was represented in 21 annotated cell groups, but only 5 groups crossed the primary FDR threshold for downstream differential expression. The strongest effect was in 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut with 155 DE genes split across both induction and repression, followed by 191 MB P MY GABA with 28 genes and 155 MB Glut with 14 genes. Sample size contributed to sensitivity (DE burden versus n_pert_matched Pearson r = 0.81), but it was not sufficient to explain the pattern: 052 Pvalb Gaba had 83 perturbed cells and no FDR-significant DE genes, whereas 191 MB P MY GABA had 52 perturbed cells and 28 DE genes.

  • 16 of 21 cell groups showed no FDR-significant downstream DE genes for Dnm1l.
  • Self-depletion of Dnm1l was strongest in 155 MB Glut (logFC -0.69, q 4.6e-5) and was also significant in 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut (logFC -0.46, q 1.4e-4).
  • The main responding labels are subcortical: thalamic, midbrain, hypothalamic, and hindbrain-associated groups.

2Cell-Type Specificity

The response is strongly cell-type selective rather than uniformly pan-neuronal. Subcortical thalamic and midbrain-associated groups dominate the signal, while cortical IT populations, hippocampal groups, common interneuron classes, and cerebellar GABA show little to no FDR-significant downstream transcriptional response. The strongest responder is not simply the largest group: some reasonably powered groups remain silent, whereas a smaller midbrain GABA group shows a robust ISR-like signature.

  • Highest DE burden: 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut (155 genes; 74 up, 81 down; n_pert_matched 158).
  • Second tier responders: 191 MB P MY GABA (28 genes; n_pert_matched 52) and 155 MB Glut (14 genes; n_pert_matched 105).
  • Small but reproducible responders: 066 CNU-HYa HY GABA (4 genes) and 145 MH-LH TH Glut (3 genes).

Atlas labels are coarse. Classical vulnerable populations discussed in the literature, such as purified Purkinje cells or dopaminergic neurons, were not directly isolated as dedicated Dnm1l-perturbed groups in this summary layer.

3Molecular Signature

Across the responsive groups, Dnm1l perturbation induces a highly coherent ISR-metabolic module centered on CHAC1, DDIT3, ASNS, amino-acid transporters, and mitochondrial one-carbon genes. Using the prespecified 12-gene module from the original report, the highest median induction appears in 191 MB P MY GABA (1.74), followed by 145 MH-LH TH Glut (1.34), 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut (1.24), 155 MB Glut (1.17), and 066 CNU-HYa HY GABA (1.15). ATF4 transcript itself rises only modestly and never reaches FDR significance, while Gdf15 and Fgf21 remain non-significant across groups.

  • Chac1, Shmt2, Slc6a9, Asns, and Cars are FDR-significant in 4 responsive groups.
  • Vegfa is significant only in 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut and 191 MB P MY GABA.
  • The supplied report did not highlight a comparably strong peroxisomal transcriptional program.

4Pathways and Biological Interpretation

The strongest thalamic responder shows a biphasic pattern: a pronounced adaptive mitochondrial-stress program turns on while neuronal, synaptic, and mitochondrial-encoded respiratory transcripts fall. In contrast, the midbrain-associated responders are dominated by ISR/amino-acid/one-carbon induction with little broad neuronal repression. This supports a staged interpretation in which some cell states remain in stress adaptation, while 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut appears to cross into deeper functional compromise.

  • Upregulated themes from the supplied report: tRNA aminoacylation, amino-acid metabolism and transport, aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis, and one-carbon/folate metabolism.
  • Downregulated themes in 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut: nervous-system development, synapse organization, axon development, and oxidative phosphorylation.
  • Representative induced genes: Stc2, Cdkn1a, Atf5, Chac1, Sesn2, Slc7a5, Mthfd2, Shmt2, and Ddit3.
  • Representative repressed genes: mt-Co2, mt-Co3, mt-Atp6, mt-Atp8, Slc17a7, Aplp1, Cdh2, Slitrk3, Tuba1a, Tubb3, Tubb2a, and Atp2a2.

5Cross-Perturbation Context

Dnm1l consistently falls into a mitochondrial/ER proteostasis neighborhood rather than behaving as an isolated signature. In 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut, Dnm1l aligns tightly with Hspa9, Opa1, Atp2a2, Hspa5, and Lonp1, and overlap with selected neighbors is directionally clean: 26 shared FDR genes with Opa1, 104 with Hspa9, and 52 with Atp2a2, all reported as same-direction in the original analysis. Negative correlations exist, but they are weaker and more heterogeneous than the positive mitochondrial-stress cluster.

  • Opa1 appears as a close neighbor in all three key responder groups.
  • In 191 MB P MY GABA, the strongest positive neighbors are Hspa9, Hspa8, Opa1, Lonp1, and Atp2a2.
  • In 155 MB Glut, the strongest positive neighbors are Opa1, Hspa9, Lonp1, and Atp2a2.

6Literature Context and Novelty

The overall transcriptomic direction agrees well with established DNM1L/DRP1 biology: mitochondrial homeostasis failure, neuronal vulnerability, and ISR-like compensation after loss of function. The dataset adds most clearly by mapping where that response is strongest across many brain cell states and by showing that the thalamic glutamatergic group can progress beyond stress adaptation into broader neuronal-program loss. It also sharpens a practical nuance for developers: these data align more naturally with genetic loss-of-function literature than with simplistic claims that less DRP1 activity is uniformly protective.

7Drug Attempts

The supplied report identifies no clinically validated DNM1L-directed therapy and no clear human interventional trial history. The asset landscape is dominated by historical probes, peptide interface blockers, and early preclinical small molecules that differ in selectivity logic but have not yet escaped specificity, delivery, or safety problems. Read together with the Dnm1l Perturb-seq signature, the main translational lesson is that indiscriminate, brain-penetrant target suppression is not de-risked.

8Clinical and Translational Relevance

The supplied literature context makes human DNM1L a real disease gene, but these data sharpen the direction-of-effect problem for developers. In this mouse brain screen, target suppression itself activates a neuronal mitochondrial-stress program and, in the most sensitive state, erodes parts of the neuronal functional transcriptome. That makes chronic CNS inhibition look high-risk, while supporting two more credible uses: mutation-class-specific rescue in rare DNM1L disease and use of the transcriptional signature as a safety or benchmarking assay for new DRP1 modulators.

  • Best-fit development logic from the supplied report: rare-disease precision rescue, downstream mitochondrial-homeostasis rescue, or short-course tissue-targeted modulation outside chronic CNS exposure.
  • The Dnm1l signature is well suited to a neurotoxicity sentinel panel in human iPSC-derived neurons or other translational models.
  • A practical assay panel would center on CHAC1, DDIT3, ASNS, ATF5, mitochondrial one-carbon genes, and loss of synaptic/axonal genes in vulnerable neuronal states.

9Methods, Data Notes, and Caveats

Direct observations in this rerender come from explicit inspection of the supplied parquet rather than from external services. The analysis uses differential-expression summaries rather than raw counts, trajectories, or guide-level efficacy estimates, so it is strongest for ranking cell-state responses and recurrent markers rather than for causal kinetics. Power varied meaningfully across cell groups, and the atlas labels are broad enough that biologically important subtypes can be diluted within a larger class.

Judge Scores

Novelty6
Novelty rationale

Most of the core story recapitulates established DNM1L/DRP1 biology: mitochondrial dysfunction, ISR-like activation, and neuronal vulnerability after loss of function are already well supported in the literature. The genuinely additive parts are the brain-wide single-cell map of response heterogeneity, the specific recurrent ISR/one-carbon/amino acid transport module, the stronger thalamic glutamatergic 'second-threshold' signature with synaptic and mt-encoded transcript loss, and the perturbational convergence with Opa1/Hspa9/Lonp1/Atp2a2. Those are useful and reasonably specific extensions, but they mostly refine rather than overturn prior understanding, and some broader mechanistic links remain inferential.

Biological Significance8
Biological Significance rationale

If valid, the findings are biologically important because DNM1L sits at a central mitochondrial-control node and the reports identify selective neuronal contexts in which its perturbation triggers a strong stress program and possible functional compromise. The signatures are coherent, internally consistent, and aligned with known mitochondrial stress biology, making the subcortical selectivity and thalamic severity potentially meaningful for brain vulnerability. The score is not higher because the evidence is still transcriptomic and cross-sectional, with limited direct demonstration of downstream physiology, degeneration, or circuit-level consequences.

Clinical Interest7
Clinical Interest rationale

DNM1L appears clinically relevant because it is already a bona fide human neurologic disease gene, the tissue of interest is highly disease-relevant, and the reports provide a plausible translational use for the signature as an on-target CNS liability readout. The clinical extension also appropriately reframes the target: simple chronic brain-penetrant inhibition looks risky, whereas mutation-specific rescue or pathology-selective, short-course modulation are more credible paths. Clinical attractiveness is moderated by broad tissue essentiality, direction-of-effect heterogeneity across variants, lack of mature human-tested modulators, and the fact that parts of the development landscape rely on preclinical or summary/snippet-level evidence rather than robust clinical validation.
