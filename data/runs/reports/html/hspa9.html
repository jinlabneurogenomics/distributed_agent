Hspa9 perturbation reveals selective neuronal stress vulnerability

In this mouse brain Perturb-seq dataset, Hspa9 perturbation does not produce a uniform pan-neuronal effect. It instead triggers a sharp, interpretable stress-response program in a subset of neuronal populations, led by thalamic and midbrain glutamatergic cells and Pvalb interneurons, with clear implications for target safety and context-dependent biology.

Executive Summary

Biology

Hspa9 perturbation generated a recurrent ISR/proteostasis signature centered on Shmt2, Chac1, Ddit3, Herpud1, Asns, and Slc7a5, together with loss of neuronal-function genes such as Kifc2, Dnm1, Gabrb2, and Hrh3. The strongest response was in 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut with 649 FDR<0.05 genes, followed by 155 MB Glut with 87 and 052 Pvalb Gaba with 30.

Clinical

As summarized in the source report, HSPA9 already carries human disease, developmental, marrow, and renal safety concerns. This dataset adds a concrete CNS-liability signal by showing selective transcriptional stress in subcortical glutamatergic neurons and Pvalb interneurons rather than a bland, ubiquitous response.

Decision Signal

For development decisions, HSPA9 looks biologically real but likely narrow-therapeutic-index. The data fit BBB-sparing, biomarker-guided oncology concepts better than systemic or CNS-directed inhibition.

1Perturbation Overview

Across 355,046 Hspa9 rows spanning 21 cell groups and 17,988 measured genes, the perturbation produced a sharply non-uniform transcriptional phenotype. Nine groups had at least one FDR<0.05 differentially expressed gene, but the burden was dominated by 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut with 649 genes, followed by 155 MB Glut with 87 and 052 Pvalb Gaba with 30. Hspa9 mRNA itself was not significantly downregulated in any group in this table, so the readout is best interpreted as the downstream consequence of functional perturbation rather than confirmed transcript depletion.

  • Top FDR<0.05 responders were 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut (649 genes), 155 MB Glut (87), 052 Pvalb Gaba (30), and 145 MH-LH TH Glut (11).
  • Twelve of 21 groups had 0 FDR<0.05 genes despite measurable perturbed-cell counts.
  • The strongest recurrent program combined stress-gene induction with selective loss of neuronal and trafficking genes rather than a diffuse pan-mitochondrial shutdown.

2Cell-Type Specificity

Response magnitude was only weakly related to the number of perturbed cells, arguing that cell identity rather than sampling depth drove the phenotype. The most sensitive classes were subcortical glutamatergic neurons and Pvalb interneurons, while many cortical IT, hippocampal, and several other GABAergic groups remained largely transcriptionally quiet despite comparable cell numbers. A few low-burden groups still showed partial stress-marker induction, which suggests a gradient of susceptibility rather than a simple binary split.

  • Spearman correlation between perturbed-cell count and FDR<0.05 DE burden across 21 groups was rho=0.11 (p=0.62).
  • Example contrast: 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut had 103 perturbed cells and 649 DE genes, whereas 005 L4-5 IT CTX Glut had 93 perturbed cells and 0.
  • 110 CNU-HYa HY MM Glut showed only 3 FDR<0.05 genes but still had Ddit3, Chac1, and Herpud1 at FDR<0.1, consistent with weak engagement of the same stress axis.

3Molecular Signature

Where Hspa9 perturbation registered, it repeatedly induced a compact stress-adaptation program rather than a generic collapse of all mitochondrial transcripts. The most recurrent upregulated genes were Shmt2, Chac1, Ddit3, Herpud1, Eprs, Asns, Slc7a1, and Slc7a5, while recurrent decreases centered on Kifc2, Dnm1, Rap1gap, Gabrb2, and Hrh3. The strongest cell types therefore combined organelle-stress signaling with loss of neuronal transport and synaptic programs.

  • 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut: top up genes by score included Shmt2, Cyb5r1, Chac1, Zfand2b, Cdk5r1, Slc6a9, Slc7a1, Herpud1; top down genes included Kifc2, Plekhg1, Tuba1a, Adgrb1, Peg3, Tubb3, Stmn3, Rhbdl1.
  • 155 MB Glut: top up genes included Herpud1, Cyb5r1, Taf15, Nars, Eprs, Chac1, Angptl6, Shmt2; top down genes included Kifc2, Kcnt1, Rtn3, Cstf2, Plppr3, Epb41, Thy1, Dbn1.
  • 052 Pvalb Gaba: top up genes included Slc6a9, Slc7a5, Lonp1, Cyb5r1, Syt4, Herpud1, Slc3a2, Nars; top down genes included Kifc2, Cacna2d2, Ppfia4, Hdac3, Tnrc6a, Amph, Grik2, Hrh3.

4Pathways and Biological Interpretation

The source report's prior enrichment analysis mapped the recurrent upregulated genes to amino-acid transport, aminoacyl-tRNA synthesis, and unfolded/integrated stress-response modules, while the strongest downregulated component covered neuronal system, synaptic signaling, membrane-potential regulation, and microtubule trafficking. Combined with the direct gene-level observations, the most parsimonious reading is organelle proteostasis stress followed by suppression of mature neuronal function in the most vulnerable cells. The original report's literature framing is consistent with HSPA9/mortalin as mitochondrial HSP70 and with an ATF4/CHOP-centered stress program.

  • Dataset-derived transport module: Slc7a5, Slc7a1, Slc1a4, Slc38a1, and Slc3a2 support adaptive amino-acid import.
  • Dataset-derived translational-stress module: Aars, Cars, Eprs, Nars, and related synthetases support tRNA charging / translational rewiring.
  • Dataset-derived ISR/proteostasis module: Chac1, Ddit3, Herpud1, Lonp1, Shmt2, and Asns recur across sensitive cell classes.
  • Dataset-derived neuronal loss module: Kifc2, Dnm1, Gabrb2, Hrh3, and Rap1gap fit reduced synaptic, excitability, and trafficking programs.
  • Hypothesis carried forward from the source report: ER-mitochondria calcium handling may contribute to the phenotype, supported indirectly by transcriptomic proximity to Atp2a2 but not directly measured here.

5Cross-Perturbation Context

Within the strongest thalamic population, the Hspa9 signature clustered tightly with perturbations affecting proteostasis, mitochondrial dynamics, calcium handling, and biosynthetic stress. Positive correlations were led by Elp1, Hspa5, Gfpt1, Dhdds, Atp2a2, Dnm1l, Opa1, Hspa8, and Psmb4; in 155 MB Glut the nearest neighbors were Atp2a2, Dnm1l, Hspa8, Opa1, Elp1, Dhdds, Psmb4, and Gfpt1. Anticorrelated signatures existed but were much weaker, which emphasizes convergence onto a shared stress state rather than a strong opposing transcriptional program.

  • Correlation in this rendering was recomputed within each cell type as Pearson correlation of Wilcoxon score vectors across genes significant for Hspa9 at FDR<0.05.
  • In 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut, several canonical mitochondrial proteostasis perturbations highlighted in the source report-Hspd1, Clpp, Clpx, Dnajc19, Afg3l2, and Lonp1-had 0 FDR<0.05 genes despite 137 to 270 perturbed cells.

6Literature Context and Novelty

Most of the observed transcriptional biology is directionally consistent with established HSPA9/mortalin literature rather than overturning it. The recurring CHAC1-DDIT3-HERPUD1-SHMT2-LONP1-like axis, the fit to mitochondrial proteostasis stress, and the loss of neuronal-function genes all align with the source report's literature framing around mtHsp70 biology. The more novel contribution of this dataset is the single-cell vulnerability map: specific subcortical glutamatergic populations and Pvalb interneurons appear much more sensitive than many cortical IT and hippocampal classes.

  • Literature-aligned: HSPA9/mortalin is a mitochondrial HSP70 chaperone involved in protein import, folding, and quality control.
  • Literature-aligned: the source report links CHAC1, DDIT3, SHMT2, HERPUD1, and LONP1 to an ATF4/CHOP-centered mitochondrial stress response.
  • Literature-aligned: transcriptomic proximity to Dnm1l and Opa1 fits prior narratives around mitochondrial dynamics and neuronal stress resilience.
  • Potentially newer dataset-level insight: subcortical glutamatergic neurons and Pvalb interneurons are more transcriptionally fragile than many cortical IT populations despite similar perturbation counts.
  • Expression context carried forward from the source report: HSPA9 is broadly expressed, so the selective response likely reflects context-dependent fragility rather than tissue-restricted target presence.

Novelty should be interpreted cautiously because the current evidence is transcriptomic only, and vulnerability is inferred from expression change burden rather than direct functional decline.

7Drug Attempts

Therapeutic history around HSPA9 is real but still early, with no approved HSPA9-directed medicines identified in the source report. The most definitive clinical readout is negative: the first-wave compound MKT-077 reached phase I but was stopped for renal toxicity. Later programs show preclinical activity or active early clinical testing, but remain limited by selectivity, incomplete validation, or broader HSP70-network pharmacology.

  • The source report found a history dominated by inhibition or disruption of mortalin/Hsp70 interactions; mature rescue or augmentation programs were not identified.
  • The translational signal is therefore mixed: proof of pharmacologic tractability exists, but therapeutic window and mechanism selectivity remain major obstacles.

8Clinical and Translational Relevance

For a drug developer, this perturbation result is more useful as a liability map than as support for safe CNS inhibition. The source report already argued that HSPA9 sits in a narrow-window zone because of human disease associations, mouse essentiality, marrow sensitivity, and historic renal toxicity; the new brain dataset adds selective neuronal stress in subcortical glutamatergic and Pvalb populations. The most plausible opportunity remains biomarker-selected oncology with tight exposure control and minimized CNS penetration.

  • Dataset-based CNS liability signal: strongest effects in 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut, 155 MB Glut, 052 Pvalb Gaba, and 145 MH-LH TH Glut.
  • Monitoring priorities carried forward from the source report: kidney, marrow / anemia-related readouts, and neurotoxicity.
  • Reasonable efficacy hypotheses from the source report include MAPK-high tumors, BRAF/KRAS-driven settings, and medullary thyroid carcinoma / RET-resistant biology.
  • For rare loss-of-function disease, the biologically coherent direction is rescue or augmentation rather than inhibition.

9Methods, Data Notes, and Caveats

This rendering used the supplied report as the narrative backbone and directly re-inspected differential-expression dataset for concrete values, tables, and figures. All group-level summaries are restricted to rows with gene_target = Hspa9. DE counts use adjusted P values from the parquet, while recurrence tables use FDR<0.1 where noted to capture weaker but repeated stress-marker induction.

  • Subset inspected: 355,046 rows across 21 cell groups and 17,988 genes.
  • Primary DE threshold in overview tables: FDR<0.05; recurrence and marker tallies: FDR<0.1 where explicitly stated.
  • Cross-perturbation correlations in this report were recomputed within a cell type as Pearson correlations of Wilcoxon score vectors across genes significant for Hspa9 in that cell type.
  • Large single-cell log fold-change estimates can arise from sparse genes, so interpretation prioritized adjusted P value, recurrence across cell types, and score concordance.
  • Hspa9 transcript itself was not significantly reduced in any cell type in this DE table.
  • Literature and drug-development statements were inherited from the source report and not re-queried on the web during this rendering step.

Transcriptional burden is an indirect proxy for vulnerability. No direct measurements of mitochondrial physiology, neuronal firing, morphology, or cell survival are available in this dataset.

Judge Scores

Novelty5
Novelty rationale

The core biology reported—mitochondrial/organellar stress, ATF4-CHOP-like ISR activation, amino acid transport and tRNA charging genes, and reduced neuronal/synaptic programs after Hspa9 perturbation—largely fits established HSPA9/mortalin literature rather than overturning it. The moderately novel piece is the in vivo single-cell mapping of a non-uniform response, especially stronger signatures in specific subcortical glutamatergic populations and Pvalb interneurons compared with many cortical/hippocampal classes, plus the comparison to other perturbations in the same dataset. The score is limited by the lack of orthogonal validation and a few speculative extensions, such as ER-mitochondrial calcium involvement and secondary-target claims.

Biological Significance7
Biological Significance rationale

If the observations are valid, they point to Hspa9 as an important determinant of neuronal mitochondrial proteostasis and stress resilience in vivo. A strong, coherent ISR/UPR-like transcriptional response together with loss of axonal/synaptic programs in selected neuron classes would be biologically meaningful, especially given HSPA9's central role in mitochondrial protein import/folding and prior links to neuronal vulnerability. The score is not higher because the evidence is transcriptomic rather than functional, 'vulnerability' is inferred mainly from DE burden, and on-target perturbation is inferred from downstream signatures rather than direct Hspa9 mRNA depletion in the DE table.

Clinical Interest7
Clinical Interest rationale

The combined reports make HSPA9 clinically interesting but difficult. There is real translational grounding: human disease associations, mouse essentiality, hematologic/developmental relevance, preclinical oncology dependency data, a failed first-generation Phase I molecule, and an ongoing early clinical program involving mortalin/HSP70-network modulation. The new brain dataset adds meaningful CNS liability context, which increases relevance for drug development decisions even though it weakens enthusiasm for systemic inhibition. The score is held below the top tier because selectivity, druggability, and therapeutic window remain major obstacles and much of the public chemistry is still early or nonselective.
