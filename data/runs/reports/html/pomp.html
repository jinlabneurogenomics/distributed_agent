Pomp perturbation reveals selective neuronal proteasome-failure states

Pomp perturbation in mouse brain neurons maps cleanly to a proteasome-failure state. The strongest signal is subcortical, with stress and proteasome-compensation genes rising while synaptic and excitability genes fall, and with tight internal similarity to proteasome-subunit perturbations.

Executive Summary

Biology

Pomp perturbation produces a proteostasis-failure signature dominated by stress-gene induction and synaptic-gene repression, with the strongest effects in midbrain glutamatergic and hypothalamic GABAergic neurons.

Clinical

Human genetics already make POMP more compelling as a liability or rescue target than as a tractable inhibitory target; the mouse brain data add a neuronal liability signal that argues against CNS-exposed suppression.

Decision Signal

Use POMP as a mechanistic and translational risk node rather than a routine direct-target program. The clearest opportunities are mutation-aware rescue/correction in rare disease and indirect oncology combinations for proteasome-inhibitor resistance.

1Perturbation Overview

Within the staged parquet, the Pomp subset contains 355,046 rows across 21 cell-type groups. Only 7 cell types show any FDR-significant differential expression, and just 4 have at least 10 FDR-significant genes, indicating a concentrated rather than ubiquitous response. The core pattern is proteostasis and stress genes up, neuronal maintenance genes down. Pomp mRNA itself is not FDR-significantly depleted in any cell type, so the evidence rests on the trans-signature.

  • Most vulnerable populations are subcortical, led by 155 MB Glut and 066 CNU-HYa HY GABA.
  • There are 1,228 unique genes significant at FDR < 0.05 in at least one cell type.
  • All 21 Pomp transcript rows have adjusted p values of 1.0.

2Cell-Type Specificity

The response is sharply cell-type selective. 155 MB Glut is by far the strongest cell type with 1,149 FDR-significant genes, and 066 CNU-HYa HY GABA is the clear second at 213. This is not simply a power effect: 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut has 83 perturbed cells but only 4 FDR genes, whereas 066 CNU-HYa HY GABA has 54 perturbed cells and 213 FDR genes.

  • Pomp ranks 6/2041 by DE burden in 155 MB Glut and 6/2023 in 066 CNU-HYa HY GABA.
  • 052 Pvalb Gaba shows a smaller but related synaptic-release phenotype.
  • Many cortical and hippocampal populations show only weak raw-p consistency.

3Molecular Signature

The molecular response is a stress-up / synaptic-down program. In 155 MB Glut, Atf3 (+6.57), Cdkn1a (+4.44), Hmox1 (+4.20), Ddit3 (+1.67), and proteasome genes such as Psmd4 (+0.81) rise, while Atp1a3 (-1.07), Slc17a6 (-1.05), Gria2 (-1.00), Syp (-0.87), and Grin1 (-0.66) fall. 066 CNU-HYa HY GABA shows the same polarity with Hmox1 (+4.91), Cdkn1a (+2.99), and Psmd4 (+0.74) up and Vamp1 (-1.22), Gria2 (-0.90), Syp (-0.84), and Atp1a3 (-0.77) down.

4Pathways and Biological Interpretation

Directly from the dataset, Pomp perturbation looks like a proteasome-assembly defect followed by compensatory proteostasis activation and loss of neuronal maintenance. The upregulated side is rich in proteasome, UPR, oxidative-stress, and injury markers, while the downregulated side is rich in synaptic vesicle, receptor, and excitability genes. In literature context, this matches the known role of POMP in proteasome maturation and prior reports of CHOP/UPR activation under POMP insufficiency.

  • Induced stress-state markers: Hmox1, Cdkn1a, Ddit3, Bax, Sqstm1, Psmd4, Psmc6.
  • Repressed neuronal-liability markers: Atp1a3, Atp1b1, Gria2, Grin1, Syp, Sv2a, Vamp1.
  • Tnfrsf12a/Fn14 rises to +2.16 in 155 MB Glut and is a plausible secondary injury node.

6Literature Context and Novelty

Most of the biology agrees with established POMP literature: impaired proteasome maturation, CHOP/UPR activation, and compensatory proteostasis signaling are all expected. The dataset adds value by placing that biology in an in vivo neuronal setting and by showing that vulnerability is strongly cell-type selective despite broad baseline expression. It also diverges from the human immune-syndrome literature by showing a neuronal program dominated by proteotoxic stress and synaptic failure rather than broad interferon activation.

  • The most novel element is selective subcortical vulnerability.
  • Only limited immune-like induction is seen: in 155 MB Glut, Tap1 is +1.61 (FDR 0.0038) and Tapbp +0.88 (FDR 0.0055), but Ifit1, Ifit3, Isg15, Stat1, and Irf7 are not FDR-significant.

7Drug Attempts

For development, POMP currently looks like validated biology rather than a mature drug target. The strongest human treatment precedent is hematopoietic rescue for PRAID, while oncology evidence points to indirect or RNA-based suppression of the NRF2/POMP/proteasome axis as a chemosensitization strategy. The supplied report found no credible public direct-binder program.

  • Successful treatment history is compartment rescue, not direct POMP pharmacology.
  • The oncology angle is combination or resistance reversal, not monotherapy.

8Clinical and Translational Relevance

The combined transcriptomic and literature picture argues against systemic POMP inhibition and especially against CNS-exposed POMP-lowering strategies. Human genetics already flag skin and immune liabilities, and this dataset adds a neuronal liability signal with a strong subcortical stress-and-synaptic-failure state. The more credible opportunities are mutation-aware rescue or correction in rare disease and indirect combination strategies in proteasome-inhibitor-resistant oncology.

  • Potential pharmacodynamic markers: Hmox1, Atf3, Cdkn1a, Ddit3, Sqstm1, Psmd4, Psmc6.
  • Potential neuronal safety markers: Atp1a3, Syp, Sv2a, Gria2, Grin1, Vamp1.
  • Any POMP-lowering oncology concept should minimize BBB exposure.

9Methods, Data Notes, and Caveats

This structured report was reconstructed from the supplied narrative and direct inspection of the staged parquet. The analysis subset used gene_target = Pomp only, with adjusted p value as the primary significance threshold and raw-p recurrence as a secondary screen. Cross-perturbation similarity was recalculated within cell type using Pearson correlation over genes that were FDR-significant for Pomp in that cell type.

Judge Scores

Novelty5
Novelty rationale

The reports mostly reinforce established POMP biology: impaired proteasome maturation, ER/proteotoxic stress, and compensatory proteasome-gene induction are all well aligned with prior literature. The main genuinely additive piece is the in vivo neuronal context, especially the apparent cell-type-selective vulnerability of subcortical neurons and the linked synaptic/excitability-gene suppression program, plus the observation that neuronal responses are not dominated by the broad interferon phenotype emphasized in human immune syndromes. Novelty is limited by the fact that the core signature is highly expected for proteasome dysfunction, Pomp mRNA itself was not measurably depleted, and some mechanistic interpretations are plausible but not directly demonstrated.

Biological Significance6
Biological Significance rationale

If the observations are real, they matter biologically: the reports connect loss of a core proteasome assembly factor to selective neuronal stress responses and loss of mature synaptic/homeostatic programs in vivo, with strong similarity to proteasome-subunit perturbations. That has clear relevance to neuronal proteostasis and selective vulnerability. The score is held to the moderate range because the evidence is transcriptomic only, strongest in a limited subset of cell types, and does not yet show direct proteasome impairment, protein-level changes, or functional neural phenotypes.

Clinical Interest6
Clinical Interest rationale

Clinically, POMP is more than a speculative target because it is already a bona fide human disease gene and is linked to defined syndromes, transplant-based therapeutic response, and preclinical oncology-resistance biology. The reports also sensibly frame the likely translational directions: mutation-aware rescue/correction in rare disease and indirect modulation of the NRF2/POMP/proteasome axis in proteasome-inhibitor-resistant cancer. Still, direct targetability appears poor, public chemistry is essentially absent, and systemic or CNS-exposed POMP inhibition looks more like a liability than a therapeutic opportunity. So the target has real but constrained clinical pull.
