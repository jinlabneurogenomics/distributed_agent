Hspa8 perturbation in mouse-brain Perturb-seq

Across 21 analyzed mouse-brain cell groups, Hspa8 perturbation produced a small but coherent proteostasis-stress signature rather than a global transcriptional collapse. The response was strongest in a thalamic Prkcd/Grin2c glutamatergic population, echoed directionally in several additional classes, and occurred without significant Hspa8 mRNA depletion, so the result is best read as a partial functional-perturbation signature with clear CNS inhibition caution.

Executive Summary

Biology

Hspa8 perturbation yielded only 13 FDR-significant differential-expression rows across the dataset, but those hits and many sub-threshold trends converged on a recurring chaperone/co-chaperone program centered on Dnaja1, Hsph1, Hsp90aa1/ab1, Dnajb1, and Stip1. The clearest cell-type-specific response was in 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut, where proteostasis stress coincided with changes in neuronal-state genes including Unc79 and Apba2.

Clinical

For a drug developer, this profile reads more like insufficiency of a neuronal support factor than a clean inhibitory target. The dataset therefore argues against chronic CNS HSPA8 inhibition and supports use of HSPA8-linked stress markers as translational readouts; the supplied literature context points more favorably to augmentation/proteostasis-support strategies in neurology and conditional inhibition mainly in selected oncology settings.

Decision Signal

Advance HSPA8 primarily as an augmentation/biomarker node or tightly selected combination target, not as a straightforward brain inhibition program. The biological signal is coherent enough to matter, but absent self-knockdown and sparse FDR support keep confidence at a moderate rather than de-risked level.

1Perturbation Overview

The source parquet contains 745,758,369 differential-expression rows, and Hspa8 perturbation is represented in 21 of 23 broad brain cell groups. Across those groups, perturbed-cell counts ranged from 28 to 115, but only 13 differential-expression rows reached FDR < 0.05, spanning 11 distinct genes. The most important quality finding is that the Hspa8 self-row never reached significance in any analyzed group, even though 2,206 self-target rows from other perturbations do so elsewhere in the screen.

  • Hspa8 perturbation is absent from 215 MB Dopa and 217 P MY Pineal Glut, so dopaminergic and pineal contexts cannot be evaluated here.
  • The most negative Hspa8 self-row was in 308 CB GABA (score -2.65, logFC -0.56, FDR 1.0), still far from significance.
  • The direct dataset signal is therefore better interpreted as a partial or functional perturbation state than as a verified full-loss-of-function transcriptomic profile.

2Cell-Type Specificity

The Hspa8 response is sharply concentrated: only three cell groups show any FDR-supported differential expression, with 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut accounting for 9 of the 13 significant rows. The two other responsive groups are 066 CNU-HYa HY GABA and 009 L2-3 IT PIR AON ENT Glut, each with 2 significant genes. This concentration is not explained by simple power alone, because the relationship between perturbed-cell count and significant-gene count across the 21 groups is weak (Spearman rho = 0.10, p = 0.665).

  • 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut is the only group with a multi-gene FDR-supported signature, combining proteostasis induction with downregulation of neuronal-state genes.
  • 066 CNU-HYa HY GABA shows a compact response consisting of Hsp90aa1 upregulation and Taf2 downregulation.
  • 009 L2-3 IT PIR AON ENT Glut shows Dnaja1 and Mars1 upregulation, consistent with chaperone plus translational-stress signaling.
  • Exploratory 8-gene core-marker mean scores suggest weaker directional echoes in 191 MB P MY GABA (2.84) and 155 MB Glut (2.74) despite no FDR-significant genes.

4Pathways and Biological Interpretation

The most defensible pathway-level interpretation is compensatory proteostasis or heat-shock-like activation after partial Hspa8 functional impairment, consistent with the supplied literature on HSPA8/HSC70 as a constitutive HSP70-family chaperone involved in protein quality control, autophagy, and vesicle trafficking [R1,R2,R4,R6]. In the strongest thalamic response, that proteostasis program is coupled to decreased neuronal-state genes Unc79 and Apba2 and increased Syt4, suggesting early effects on excitability or synaptic-state machinery rather than indiscriminate transcriptional failure. A layered ISR-style component is plausible but less secure than the core chaperone signal [R15].

  • Proteostasis module: Dnaja1, Hspa4l, Hsp90aa1, Hsp90ab1, Dnajb1, Stip1, Hsph1, and Cct6a support a compensatory chaperone-network interpretation [R4,R14].
  • Neuron-state features in 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut include Unc79 down (score -4.34, logFC -0.57, FDR 0.035), Apba2 down (score -4.23, logFC -0.44, FDR 0.045), and Syt4 up (score 5.14, logFC 0.56, FDR 0.0016) [R7,R8,R9].
  • Suggestive ISR markers include Ddit3 in 066 CNU-HYa HY GABA (score 3.73, logFC 1.48, FDR 0.410) and recurrent appearance of Asns, Chac1, and aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases [R15].

5Cross-Perturbation Context

Within 151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut, the Hspa8 signature is most similar to perturbations affecting proteostasis and quality-control machinery rather than to a broad generic stress background. Whole-transcriptome correlations are strongest for Hspa9, Psmc5, Psmb4, Elp1, Uba5, and Ufc1, while the negative side of the correlation distribution is shallow. Hsf1 is the clearest biologically interpretable anti-signal only when the comparison is focused on the recurrent Hspa8 chaperone-gene panel rather than on the entire transcriptome.

  • Top positive whole-transcriptome correlations versus Hspa8 in 151 are Hspa9 (r=0.334), Psmc5 (0.290), Psmb4 (0.289), Elp1 (0.282), Uba5 (0.228), and Ufc1 (0.227).
  • Top negative whole-transcriptome correlations are shallow: Ncam1 (-0.081), Nr1h5 (-0.081), Bap1 (-0.070), Pten (-0.067), and Calm3 (-0.063).

  • Hsp90ab1 perturbation shows much cleaner transcript self-suppression than Hspa8 (15/23 groups with self FDR < 0.05 versus 0/21 for Hspa8), but in the 151 group its FDR-supported signal is dominated by Hsp90ab1 itself, arguing that the Hspa8 state is not simply generic chaperone loss.

6Literature Context and Novelty

The supplied literature makes the direction of the core result unsurprising: HSPA8/HSC70 is a broadly expressed housekeeping chaperone tied to proteostasis, CMA, and vesicle traffic, so compensatory induction of other chaperones after perturbation is biologically expected [R1,R2,R3,R4,R6]. The more novel element is not the existence of stress biology per se, but its concentration in specific neuronal populations and its pairing with neuron-state genes such as Unc79 and Apba2. That said, novelty is constrained by the small number of significant hits and the lack of verified Hspa8 transcript knockdown.

  • Human Protein Atlas context argues against a region-restricted baseline expression explanation: HSPA8 is summarized as broadly expressed with low tissue and brain regional specificity [R3].
  • The dataset adds a selective-vulnerability nuance: despite housekeeping-like expression, the strongest transcriptomic consequence appears in a thalamic Prkcd/Grin2c glutamatergic population rather than uniformly across brain classes.
  • The synaptic-vesicle angle is literature-concordant because HSPA8 participates in clathrin uncoating and membrane traffic [R6], but the present evidence remains associative rather than causal.

Most of the biology here confirms expected HSPA8 stress-buffering functions; the main novelty claim is cell-class selectivity, which is interesting but not yet deeply de-risked.

7Drug Attempts

The supplied translational review depicts HSPA8 drug discovery as real but immature. Direct pharmacology is dominated by Hsp70-family tool compounds and medicinal-chemistry lineages with selectivity or toxicity liabilities, while the clearest positive clinical precedent near this biology is pathway-level chaperone augmentation rather than HSPA8-selective inhibition. No approved HSPA8-selective asset was cited in the supplied material.

  • Successful examples are pathway-adjacent or augmentation-oriented rather than direct HSPA8 suppression.
  • Most inhibitor efforts are Hsp70-family level, not isoform-selective for HSPA8, so clinical translatability and on-target attribution remain imperfect.
  • The present neuronal perturb-seq result adds a safety caution for brain-directed inhibitor programs because even this partial perturbation evokes a stress program.

8Clinical and Translational Relevance

The direct dataset argues that reducing Hspa8 function in neurons provokes a buffered proteostasis-stress state before any broad collapse, which makes chronic CNS inhibition hard to justify. Combined with the supplied external context on broad HSPA8 expression and model-system essentiality, the more plausible neurology direction is augmentation, stabilization, or pathway-level proteostasis support [R3,R16,R17,R18,R19]. Oncology remains a possible but conditional use case, especially in resistance or combination settings, yet the cited pharmacology still looks early and safety-limited [R20,R21,R22].

  • Candidate translational biomarkers from this dataset are DNAJA1, HSP90AA1, HSP90AB1, HSPH1, and STIP1, with ASNS/CHAC1/DDIT3 as optional stress-extension markers when ISR engagement is a concern.
  • Potential neuronal-liability markers are UNC79 and APBA2, because the strongest FDR-supported thalamic response couples chaperone induction to decreased excitability or synaptic-state genes.
  • For neurology, the absence of MB Dopa data keeps Parkinson-relevant interpretation indirect even though the broader CMA literature remains supportive [R12,R17].
  • For oncology, any inhibitor strategy should be framed as conditional dependency or combination therapy and should carry explicit renal and neurologic safety monitoring.

Judge Scores

Novelty4
Novelty rationale

Most of the core signal as written is a confirmation of expected HSPA8/HSC70 biology: perturbation is associated with induction of chaperones/co-chaperones and a heat-shock/proteostasis-like response, which fits established literature and the HSF1 feedback model. The potentially newer aspect is the apparent neuron-class selectivity, especially the stronger response in the TH Prkcd Grin2c glutamatergic population and the coupled changes in a few neuron-function genes. However, novelty is limited by the sparse significant output (only 13 FDR-significant genes total), the reliance on directional trends for many claims, and the major caveat that Hspa8 mRNA was not significantly reduced in any analyzed cell class, which weakens confidence that this is a clear new Hspa8 loss-of-function state rather than a partial or indirect signature.

Biological Significance5
Biological Significance rationale

If valid, the findings are biologically meaningful because they suggest that reducing HSPA8 function in vivo can trigger a coordinated proteostasis response in specific neuronal populations and may begin to affect excitability/synaptic-state programs before overt transcriptomic collapse. That matters given HSPA8's central role in proteostasis, chaperone-mediated autophagy, and vesicle trafficking. Still, the observed phenotype is modest and context-limited: strong effects are confined to a few cell classes, the overall differential-expression signal is small, and the more interesting mechanistic extensions such as integrated stress response activation, synaptic dysfunction, or broader proteostasis-network convergence are only partially supported by the data presented.

Clinical Interest6
Clinical Interest rationale

The combined reports make HSPA8 clinically interesting but clearly difficult. They give a credible translational rationale for neuronal disease relevance through proteostasis/CMA biology, PD-related literature, and SMA-linked preclinical work, while also highlighting oncology settings where HSPA8/Hsp70 inhibition may act as a conditional dependency or combination strategy. At the same time, the reports appropriately show why enthusiasm should be tempered: HSPA8 is broadly expressed, appears functionally important in normal tissues, direct inhibition in brain looks liability-prone, selective drug development is immature, and much of the pharmacology cited comes from family-level or tool compounds with toxicity concerns rather than de-risked HSPA8-specific assets. That supports a moderate-above-average clinical score, especially for augmentation/biomarker use or selected oncology combinations, not a highly compelling near-term target score.
