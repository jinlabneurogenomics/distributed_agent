# Reasoning trace

## Question and scope

The requested manifest contains one held-out target:

| target_name |
|---|
| Tef |

I interpreted the task as a point prediction for the number of genes that would
pass `pvals_adj < 0.05` in the held-out `Tef` perturbation in `151 TH Prkcd
Grin2c Glut`. `Tef` was absent from both supplied training inputs, so the
prediction had to be transferred from non-held-out perturbations in the same
experiment rather than from a measured `Tef` signature.

The main ambiguity was whether to treat `Tef` as another core circadian-clock
perturbation or as a downstream D-box output transcription factor. I resolved
that biologically as the latter: TEF is a PAR bZip D-box activator downstream of
the BMAL1:CLOCK positive limb and parallel to DBP and HLF, whereas BMAL1,
CLOCK, REV-ERB, and ROR losses change upstream feedback loops as well as D-box
output.

  non-held-out perturbations.
- `151_TH_Prkcd_Grin2c_Glut_deg_counts_training.csv`: the observed FDR<0.05 DEG
  counts for 1,674 non-held-out targets in the focal cell group.
- Distributed Agents corpus MCP, release `perturbai-july-260713-sourceguard-1773-v1`:
  same-experiment bottom-up findings for the observed `Bmal1`, `Clock`,
  `Cry1`, `Cry2`, `Nr1d1`, `Nr1d2`, `Rora`, `Rorb`, `Rorc`, and `Bmal2`
  perturbations.
- Distributed Agents runtime profile comparison over the focal parquet rows, using
  Wilcoxon `scores` as the signed profile and `pvals_adj < 0.05` as the DEG
  threshold.
- External mechanism literature:
  - Gachon et al. 2004, Genes & Development, DOI `10.1101/gad.301404`,
    PMID `15175240`: DBP, HLF, and TEF are the three circadian PAR bZip factors;
    triple PAR bZip knockout mice have epilepsy, and `Pdxk` is a PAR bZip
    target in liver and brain.
  - Ueda et al. 2005, Nature Genetics, DOI `10.1038/ng1504`, PMID `15665827`:
    mammalian circadian transcriptional circuits use E-box, D-box
    DBP/E4BP4-binding, and RORE elements, with D-box output generated by an
    antiphasic activator/repressor mechanism.
  - Kumaki et al. 2008, PNAS, DOI `10.1073/pnas.0802636105`, PMID `18815372`:
    computational and experimental promoter analysis supported DBP/E4BP4
    binding elements as high-amplitude clock-output cis elements.
  - Yoshitane et al. 2019, Communications Biology, DOI
    `10.1038/s42003-019-0522-3`, PMID `31428688`: DBP and E4BP4 share many
    liver D-box binding sites and competitively activate or repress D-box
    output, illustrating the D-box branch downstream of the clock.

## Computation over the training data

The endpoint table had 1,674 training targets; 469 had a nonzero count and the
median target had `0` DEGs in this cell group. The focal parquet rows were read
only for clock/ROR comparator targets, avoiding a scan of all 616,253,184
parquet rows:

```python
filt = (pc.field("group_name") == "151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut") & \
       pc.field("gene_target").isin(targets)
dataset.to_table(
    columns=["names", "scores", "logfoldchanges", "pvals_adj",

```

This exactly reproduced the focal DEG counts in the CSV for the comparators:

The runtime profile comparison over the same focal rows made the same point in
score-vector space. `Bmal1`, `Nr1d1`, and `Rora` were the only broad responders
among this comparator set; `Clock`, `Cry1`, `Cry2`, and `Nr1d2` were 1-to-9-gene
responses; `Bmal2`, `Rorb`, and `Rorc` had no significant features. Whole-vector
cosine similarity between `Bmal1` and `Clock` was only `0.201`, between `Clock`
and `Cry2` only `0.064`, and between `Rora` and `Rorb` only `0.042`, reinforcing
the Distributed Agents corpus finding that the observed clock/ROR nodes are
target-specific rather than interchangeable.

## Target-specific reasoning for Tef

### Directly comparable training perturbations

The most natural direct analogs would be the three PAR bZip/D-box factors
`Dbp`, `Tef`, and `Hlf`, and the opposing D-box repressor `Nfil3`; all four
were absent as perturbation targets in the supplied training count table. That
removes the cleanest within-family transfer.

The next-best internal evidence is upstream or feedback clock perturbation:

- `Clock`, the most direct positive-limb comparator, had only 3 DEGs in the
  focal group. Importantly, one of those was `Tef` itself, down with
  `logFC = -0.517` and `q = 5.35e-4`, next to stronger `Dbp` and `Nr1d2`
  repression. The Distributed Agents corpus summarized this as `Clock:F001`: Clock loss
  depressed a tiny canonical clock-output module dominated by `Nr1d2`, `Dbp`,
  `Tef`, and `Per3` in subcortical or pineal-containing bins. This proves the
  assay can see `Tef` as a CLOCK/BMAL1 output, but it also says the immediate
  upstream response was only a few genes wide.
- `Bmal1` had 103 DEGs and was the closest positive `Clock` neighbor, but the
  corpus finding `Bmal1:F007` explicitly called `Clock` a sparse phenocopy.
  `Bmal1` engaged the broader thalamic synaptic and matrix state shared with
  `Nr1d1`; that response is upstream feedback rewiring rather than the expected
  consequence of removing a single D-box output activator. `Bmal1` did not
  significantly reduce `Tef` in the focal group.
- `Cry2` also had only 3 DEGs and shared the `Dbp`/`Nr1d1` clock-output
  direction with `Bmal1`/`Clock` rather than producing a broad inverse
  de-repression. This is a good warning that the endpoint is not a simple
  activator-versus-repressor circuit diagram.
- `Cry1` and `Nr1d2` were singleton responses, and `Bmal2`, `Rorb`, and `Rorc`
  were zero. These are negative counterexamples showing that being a clock-loop
  or ROR-family target is insufficient for a broad 151 thalamic signature.
- `Nr1d1` and `Rora` were broad, but the corpus findings `Nr1d1:F008`,
  `Nr1d1:F009`, and `Rora:F005` emphasized that their large thalamic programs
  were selective and not phenocopied by nearby circadian/ROR relatives. I
  therefore used them to establish that the cell group can mount large nuclear
  receptor responses, not to scale `Tef` upward.

### Biological zero/nonzero decision

TEF sits downstream of CLOCK/BMAL1 as a PAR bZip D-box activator and is parallel
to DBP and HLF. External PAR bZip work makes a single-gene null look less
severe than a core-clock or combined-output perturbation: Gachon et al. showed
the major brain/liver phenotype in mice lacking all three DBP/HLF/TEF proteins,
not from `Tef` alone, and Ueda/Kumaki placed PAR bZip factors in a D-box output
branch opposed by E4BP4. In this focal Perturb-seq surface, the D-box branch was
visible but narrow: directly upstream `Clock` loss changed only `Dbp`, `Nr1d2`,
and `Tef`, while a related output/feedback perturbation, `Cry2`, changed only
three genes.

Because single acute loss of one redundant output activator should be more
buffered than `Clock` or `Bmal1` loss, and because there was no observed
single-PAR-bZip broad signature to transfer from, I predict that the `Tef`
perturbation will not put any measured gene below the stringent FDR 0.05
threshold.

Prediction: `Tef -> 0`.

## Limits

The weakest part of the prediction is the missing direct comparator: `Dbp`,
`Hlf`, `Nfil3`, and `Tef` themselves were all held out or absent, so the estimate
is extrapolated from upstream clock nodes. The screen was not a circadian
time-course; a `Tef` perturbation could alter phase, amplitude, or a small
D-box-dependent transcript set at another time of day without generating a
large static mean shift here. The sparse AAV-CRISPR design also averages over
partial target engagement, so a real TEF-dependent direct target might land just
above or below the BH threshold. Finally, RORA/REV-ERB/BMAL1 comparisons show
that this thalamic group can amplify selected clock perturbations into broad
non-clock synaptic and matrix states, so a nonzero `Tef` count is not
mechanistically impossible; I judged it unlikely for a single redundant PAR
bZip output factor under this exact endpoint.
