# Methods and definitions

I treated `pvals_adj` as the within-comparison Benjamini-Hochberg FDR value from the Wilcoxon test and `logfoldchanges` as log2 fold change in perturbed nuclei relative to pooled non-targeting controls. The primary evidence set was all rows with `pvals_adj <= 0.05`; this contained 99,882 significant perturbation-cell-type-gene rows across 3,538 target-by-cell comparisons. For each target I counted significant genes per cell class, directionally recurrent downstream genes, and the most significant or largest log2 effects. A recurrent signature means the same downstream gene was FDR-significant in the same direction after the same perturbation in at least three cell classes; single-cell-class signatures were retained only when the comparison contained many significant genes and the downstream genes were biologically interpretable. I did not require a hard minimum number of perturbed nuclei, but comparisons with very small `n_pert_matched`, giant low-count fold changes, or a single isolated downstream gene were down-ranked.

Literature searches used PubMed queries combining mouse gene symbols, aliases, protein complexes, and disease names, for example `SRSF1 splicing neuron`, `UBA5 UFC1 UFMylation brain encephalopathy`, `PPP2R1A neurodevelopmental disorder PP2A`, and `THAP11`. The cited literature was used only to set the prior expectation; ranking was driven by the atlas statistics. I did not rely on a primary manuscript for this atlas or on non-PubMed mirrors of it.

Findings first passed an evidence gate: relationships whose interpretation could not be supported from FDR-significant rows were excluded, and no finding with Support below 3 was placed in the ranked list. Among eligible findings, Priority was the main ranking axis, then Support, then Novelty. I favored relationships that were seen across several related perturbations or many cell classes, affected disease-relevant regulators, or exposed a specific postmitotic neuronal response rather than simply producing many generic marker changes.

The main limitations are that the test is a single-nucleus Wilcoxon test, not a guide-level or animal-level model; guide efficiency, clone structure, and mouse-to-mouse variation are not available in this parquet file. FDR was computed within each perturbation-cell-type comparison, not across the entire 745.8 million row atlas. Cell classes are broad transcriptomic groups, NeuN enrichment makes astrocyte, microglial, oligodendrocyte, and vascular responses largely inaccessible, and lowly expressed genes can show very large log2 fold changes. The perturbations are acute, mosaic AAV-CRISPR disruptions in juvenile-to-young-adult postmitotic mouse brain; modest or absent target-transcript loss is not evidence of absent protein disruption, and the signatures are not equivalent to embryonic, germline, Cre-lox, siRNA, or pharmacological perturbations. All biological interpretations below therefore require independent validation with guide-level replication, orthogonal target engagement, and protein, splicing, physiology, or histology readouts.
