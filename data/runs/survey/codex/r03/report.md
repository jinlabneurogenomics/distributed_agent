# Methods and Definitions

I scanned all 745,758,369 differential-expression rows in `wilcoxon_de_results_group_name_final_data_no_multi_guide_cells.parquet` and summarized every perturbation-by-cell-type contrast by the number of measured genes passing `pvals_adj < 0.05` and `abs(logfoldchanges) >= 0.25`. I used `pvals_adj < 1e-4` with the same effect-size cutoff as a stricter support count, prioritized contrasts with at least 50 matched perturbed nuclei unless a lower-powered contrast was explicitly part of a repeated pathway signal, and inspected the largest absolute Wilcoxon `scores` in each signature to determine whether the downstream genes formed a coherent program. The ranked findings below merge highly redundant hits when they perturb the same machinery and produce the same downstream module, but the support text still names the exact target, cell type, nucleus count, and representative downstream rows.

Literature was searched in PubMed with gene-symbol plus disease, pathway, and cell-biology terms, for example `SETD1A schizophrenia`, `Skraban WDR26`, `UBA5 UFM1`, `SRSF1 splicing neuron`, `Kabuki KMT2D`, and `RORA cerebellum autism`. I used papers about Mendelian neurodevelopmental syndromes and core pathway papers to define the prior expectation; I did not use the atlas itself as novelty support.

Ranking used the requested two-stage scheme. First, transcriptomic signatures whose interpretation would have scored below Support 3 were excluded even if the gene was famous. Eligible findings were sorted by expected scientific value: Priority was the primary key, Support the secondary key, and Novelty the tertiary key. Priority emphasizes follow-up value after independent validation, so strong disease-gene programs in postmitotic neurons outrank equally broad housekeeping signatures when the latter mostly report generic loss of proteostasis.

Major limitations are that the statistics are single-nucleus Wilcoxon tests against pooled non-targeting nuclei, not mouse-level biological replicates; guide identities are pooled within each target; guide cutting and protein loss were not directly measured; target mRNA loss is an underestimate of functional knockout because nonsense-mediated decay catches only some edited alleles; log2 fold changes for sparsely detected transcripts can be inflated; Flex snRNA-seq measures gene abundance, not splicing, translation, electrophysiology, morphology, or viability; NeuN-positive FACS undersamples non-neuronal responses; and acute juvenile postnatal mosaic CRISPR does not model embryonic, germline, aged, or chronic disease states. Each interpretation is therefore framed as a falsifiable hypothesis about a downstream program, not as proof of a direct molecular interaction.

Title: Wdr26 loss produces an unexpectedly large cerebellar GABAergic ion-channel and splicing signature
Core Idea: The CTLH E3-ligase subunit `Wdr26` had its largest atlas effect in `308 CB GABA` nuclei, where the response was dominated by decreased RNA-processing and cerebellar excitability genes plus compensatory increases in selected neuronal regulators. The observation suggests that postnatal cerebellar inhibitory neurons are acutely sensitive to WDR26 dosage and that Skraban-Deardorff syndrome may include a cerebellar GABAergic cell-autonomous transcriptional component.
Significance: Human WDR26 variants cause a seizure and intellectual-disability syndrome, but the vulnerable mature neuronal cell classes and immediate neuronal targets of WDR26-CTLH dysfunction are still poorly defined. A 1,444-gene cerebellar GABA signature gives a concrete in vivo starting point for asking whether WDR26 regulates calcium-channel, kainate-receptor, and RNA-splicing networks rather than acting only through generic ubiquitin-ligase failure.
Prior expectation: WDR26 is known as a CTLH E3-ligase subunit whose pathogenic variants impair CTLH complex assembly in Skraban-Deardorff syndrome (Gross et al., 2024, PMID:38575527), and WDR26 loss can broadly alter chromatin accessibility in cultured non-neuronal cells (Onea et al., 2025, PMID:39837355). Those data would predict transcriptional consequences of WDR26 loss, but not a cerebellar-GABA-dominant postmitotic program.
Support: `Wdr26` perturbation in `308 CB GABA` used 101 perturbed nuclei versus 8,277 controls and changed 1,444 genes at FDR < 0.05 and abs(log2FC) >= 0.25, including 292 at FDR < 1e-4. High-scoring decreased genes included `Srrm2` -0.80, FDR 1.95e-11; `Kalrn` -1.28, FDR 6.51e-10; `Luc7l2` -0.72, FDR 6.51e-10; `Cacna1g` -1.74, FDR 6.51e-10; `Grik1` -1.46, FDR 8.30e-09; `Dagla` -1.29, FDR 1.32e-08; and `Arhgap26` -1.14, FDR 2.11e-08. Related but smaller signatures appeared in `155 MB Glut` with 470 genes from 127 perturbed nuclei and in `191 MB P MY GABA` with 324 genes from 69 perturbed nuclei. The observation is strong, but the dataset cannot tell whether the cerebellar program is direct CTLH substrate control, altered chromatin access, cell-state stress, or early toxicity.
Research community: Neurodevelopmental-disorder genetics groups, cerebellar circuit labs, and ubiquitin-ligase structural biologists would be the most motivated communities; specific laboratory names would be uncertain. Natural follow-ups are AAV rescue of WDR26 or CTLH-assembly mutants in cerebellar GABA neurons, WDR26 CUT&RUN or ATAC-seq in those nuclei, Patch-seq for `Cacna1g`/`Grik1` consequences, and grants centered on CTLH-dependent control of inhibitory cerebellar maturation in seizure syndromes.

Title: Ppp2r1a loss acutely rewires thalamic and cortical synaptic gene expression
Core Idea: Loss of the PP2A scaffold gene `Ppp2r1a` produced a large transcriptional response centered on Prkcd/Grin2c thalamic glutamatergic neurons, with parallel smaller signatures in layer 4/5 and layer 2/3 cortical IT neurons and midbrain neurons. The biological interpretation is that postnatal PP2A A-alpha dosage helps maintain a differentiated synaptic state in a specific thalamic excitatory class.
Significance: PPP2R1A-related Houge-Janssens neurodevelopmental disorder includes epilepsy and structural brain defects, but most clinical mutations are missense alleles and the affected neuronal programs are largely unknown. This atlas suggests a cell class and a transcriptional module for testing how partial PP2A scaffold loss can produce network disease after neurons are postmitotic.
Prior expectation: A 2025 systematic review reported de novo pathogenic `PPP2R1A` variants in developmental delay, intellectual disability, epilepsy, and brain malformations (Lee et al., 2025, PMID:41465181). The most valuable experiments would combine cell-type-specific `Ppp2r1a` rescue, phosphoproteomics from sorted thalamic neurons, Slice-seq or Patch-seq to connect the `Sv2b`/`Snap25` changes to release probability, and comparison with human PPP2R1A missense alleles that perturb holoenzyme assembly.

Title: UFMylation genes are required for thalamic glutamatergic transcriptional homeostasis
Core Idea: Perturbing the UFM1 E1 enzyme `Uba5` caused one of the largest non-proteasomal responses in `151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut` nuclei, and perturbing the UFM1 E2 enzyme `Ufc1` produced a smaller but directionally related thalamic response. The signature included increased `Eef1a1`, `H1f4`, `Trim8`, `Chn1`, `Ina`, and `Rab6b` with decreased `Rsrp1`, `Abcc5`, `Ccdc136`, `Clk4`, and `Clk1`. The interpretation is that the UFMylation cascade buffers ER, RNA-processing, and neuronal-structure genes in mature thalamic excitatory neurons.
Significance: Biallelic UBA5 variants cause severe epileptic encephalopathy, but UFMylation has mostly been mechanistically studied through ER and ribosome-associated quality control outside defined brain cell classes. Seeing both E1 and E2 perturbations converge in the same juvenile thalamic class creates a direct in vivo system for connecting UFM1 biochemistry to seizure-relevant neuronal maintenance.
Prior expectation: UBA5 is the sole UFM1 E1 enzyme, and UBA5/UFC1 biochemistry had been worked out as a ubiquitin-like activation pathway (Gavin et al., 2014, PMID:24966333). Recent work links UFMylation to autophagy initiation through VCP/p97 modification (Wang et al., 2024, PMID:38762759). Severe UBA5 missense variants are recognized causes of neurodevelopmental disease (Ai et al., 2025, PMID:41360240), so neuronal sensitivity was expected, but the Prkcd/Grin2c thalamic bias was not.
Support: `Uba5` perturbation in `151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut` used 245 perturbed nuclei and changed 2,177 genes at the FDR/effect threshold, including 759 at FDR < 1e-4. Top rows were `Eef1a1` +0.76, FDR 4.76e-26; `H1f4` +1.73, FDR 4.76e-26; `Rsrp1` -0.44, FDR 7.65e-26; `Abcc5` -0.50, FDR 3.52e-25; `Trim8` +1.05, FDR 1.05e-23; and `Chn1` +0.63, FDR 1.10e-22. The E2 target `Ufc1` changed 470 genes in the same class from 175 perturbed nuclei, and `Uba5` also changed 587 genes in `155 MB Glut` from 177 nuclei. Because `Uba5` has a broader and stronger footprint than `Ufc1`, part of the E1 phenotype may reflect E2-independent stress or differential knockout efficiency.
Research community: UFMylation biochemists, rare epileptic encephalopathy labs, ER-quality-control groups, and thalamic seizure-circuit investigators are the likely adopters. Strong programs would test UBA5 catalytic-dead rescue in thalamic neurons, measure RPL26 and RPN1 UFMylation after in vivo CRISPR, assay ER stress and spontaneous firing, and determine whether UFMylation activators or autophagy modulators normalize the thalamic signature.

Title: Setd1a loss selectively depresses a midbrain mitochondrial and ER-maintenance gene module
Core Idea: The H3K4 methyltransferase gene `Setd1a` produced a coherent down-regulated signature in `155 MB Glut` nuclei and a second signature in `151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut`. The atlas extends this to acute in vivo postnatal loss in defined mouse midbrain and thalamic populations.

Research community: Psychiatric-genetics groups, chromatin neurobiology labs, and mitochondrial-ER quality-control researchers would all have strong incentives to pursue this. Experiments should pair thalamic and midbrain Setd1a CUT&Tag with rescue by wild-type or catalytic SET-domain mutants, mitochondrial respiration in sorted nuclei's sibling cytoplasm where feasible, and behavioral tests of adult Setd1a restoration in the same subcortical classes.

Title: Tsc1/Tsc2 perturbation activates a midbrain lysosomal and cholesterol-handling program
Core Idea: The mTORC1 repressors `Tsc1` and `Tsc2` produced their largest overlapping responses in `155 MB Glut`, with `Tsc1` also affecting Prkcd/Grin2c thalamic and hypothalamic GABA classes. The midbrain `Tsc1` response included higher `Atp6v0b`, `Lamp2`, `Insig1`, `Pik3r3`, `Rhoq`, and `Dpp7`, while neuronal membrane genes such as `Serinc1`, `App`, `Rtn1`, `Clstn1`, and `Scg5` fell. The biological hypothesis is that acute TSC loss in postnatal midbrain glutamatergic neurons drives lysosomal/autophagic and sterol feedback downstream of mTORC1 hyperactivation.
Significance: TSC is one of the most actionable neurogenetic pathways because mTOR inhibitors are clinically available. A cell-type-resolved transcriptome showing where acute sparse TSC loss has the largest postmitotic footprint can guide why some circuits remain hard to rescue and which lysosomal genes may be early pharmacodynamic readouts.
Prior expectation: TSC1/TSC2 loss activates mTORC1 in neurons and glia in tuberous sclerosis complex (Curatolo et al., 2022, PMID:35963265). In ASD models, Tsc2 heterozygosity can impair mTOR-dependent autophagy and developmental spine pruning (Tang et al., 2014, PMID:25155956). A broad lysosomal program was therefore expected; the midbrain glutamatergic enrichment was more atlas-specific.
Support: `Tsc1` perturbation in `155 MB Glut` used 166 perturbed nuclei and changed 449 genes, 120 of them at FDR < 1e-4; high-scoring rows were `Cpeb1` +1.16, FDR 2.44e-28; `Stmn4` +1.29, FDR 2.90e-28; `Atp6v0b` +0.53, FDR 1.40e-21; `Pik3r3` +1.36, FDR 2.26e-17; `Serinc1` -0.57, FDR 7.09e-17; `Lamp2` +0.78, FDR 3.16e-12; and `Insig1` +1.02, FDR 2.22e-10. `Tsc2` in the same class used 195 perturbed nuclei and changed 186 genes. `Tsc1` also changed 259 genes in `151 TH Prkcd Grin2c Glut` and 88 in `066 CNU-HYa HY GABA`. Research community: TSC and mTOR translational scientists, lysosomal biologists, and midbrain circuit labs are the obvious audience. Strong follow-up programs include pS6 and phospho-4EBP mapping after matched perturbations, rapamycin or everolimus rescue of the `Atp6v0b`/`Lamp2`/`Insig1` module, and tests of whether midbrain glutamatergic neurons contribute to TSC-associated sleep, arousal, or seizure phenotypes.
